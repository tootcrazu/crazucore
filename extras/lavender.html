2026-02-23 - Thoughts From A Park Bench About The Meaning Of Life Because It Was That Kind Of Night And I Was Listening To Just The Right Song That Hit Me In Just The Right Way.

It's kind of the biggest question we've never answered, not that I think a satisfying answer could exist. It certinately won't benefit from my opinion.

Broadly speaking, I'm not what you'd call 'much'. I don't have any big dreams, nor big achievements. No bustling social life nor promising career. My biggest claim to fame I can think of is.. being weird. Having Fun.

I often tell myself and others that that's the point of life. Having fun, I mean. I try to live by simple rules. Rule 1: Do No Harm. Rule 2: So long as you aren't conflicting with rule 1, Have Fun.

Every now and then, the whole universe crashes into me all at once. I remember again, for an instant, that it's all real somehow. Shampoo, Flowers, Old Cars, Black Holes, Nail Polish, The smell of fresh baked bread- All of it real and tangible. Isn't it insane?? Doesn't it drive you mad?? Based on our current understanding of the formation of the universe, so many things had to go right for life - let alone human life - to exist. And I'm here.

So I think that's all I can do with this life. Have fun, and make sure I don't make anyone else's day worse while I'm at it. Because I can't think of anything else.





2026-03-02 - Thoughts From A Sick Day About Boycotts, Because That's What's On My Mind At The Moment.

Boycotts have been on my mind recently. The online spaces that I passively exist in discuss and advocate for them heavily, and for good reason. Blatantly put, when the 'owners' of a work or product do bad stuff, sometimes not giving them money is one of the few effective ways you have to lessen any negative impact they may have. A prime example of this is J.K. Rowling. It's well documented that she has donated portions of her profits to anti-trans organizations, meaning that any monetary support of her works, when considered as charitably as possible, runs the risk of benefitting a transphobic cause. Scott Cawthon of Five Nights At Freddy's fame is in a similar boat, having (on at least two occasions) donated a sizable amount of his profits to many members of the american republican party, including Trump himself.

The reasoning for the boycotting in these cases is very clear. I don't support transphobia, and I definitely don't support the american republican party. I wouldn't risk giving any money to either cause, and no shiny new movies or silly toys would be enough to sway me. It doesn't help that neither franchise is exactly "peak fiction", but that's not really relevant to the point at hand.


The larger the target of the boycott is, the murkier this gets. The BDS (a Palestinian-led organization focusing on distancing people from financial support of Israel) has called for a boycott of, among many other things, Microsoft. The corporation is one of their major targets due to it's ongoing partnership with the israeli military, providing them ai and cloud services used to surveil and carry out military action against the Palestinian population. They do this completely knowingly too.

The murkiness comes in when you consider that your average person doesn't usually spend money on Microsoft products. If they are, it's only really because they're paying for an Office subscription or buying one of their very few pieces of hardware. The boycott, then, has to primarily target the section of Microsoft that consumers are engaging in of their own volition (and can easily opt out of) - Xbox. And because these are greedy businesses in the modern era, they've gobbled up a lot of other companies. Mojang, Double Fine, Activision-Blizzard, Rare, Obsidian Entertainment... None of them chose this. In all likelihood, no one working at these companies has any say in what services Microsoft does or does not provide. I don't think they could stop it if they wanted to.

The question then becomes: Are boycotts to companies this large even effective? Does Microsoft even take notice or care when some people stop buying Xbox games, or stop playing Minecraft? There are lots of different ways you can look at answering that question. You could say that it doesn't matter. That, like the other two examples before, no game should be worth the risk of your money (or money earned from selling data about you) funding a literal genocide. If you really wanted to devil's advocate that argument, you could say "well why not stop using Windows all together? They probably collect your data through that and sell it to advertisers! In fact, your work buys Microsoft products, so you should find another job!" I don't know if anyone literally thinks like this, I use it more as an example to demonstrate that if you think about it hard enough, you can probably come up with endless things you can cut out of your life in an aim to achieve some 'perfect morality'.

I don't know what the answer is here. I honestly think it might just be down to personal preference? As odd as it sounds. How much of your money going to evil causes are you willing to tolerate? It sounds bad, but that's kind of.. how the world works a little bit? Avoiding it all would be a full time job, so best to keep informed of major causes, or ones close to your heart, and work from there. Not to say you shouldn't at least try to convince others of course, but try not to be too judgemental of the misinformed.


I'll admit, the main reason I've been thinking about this is because I kind of want to play Minecraft. It feels very.. selfish to say. After all, it's only a video game, and not even a very good one. I've only been without it for just shy of a year, and I can't say I've missed it all that much in the intervening time.
But seeing some people who are most certainly aware of the boycott play the game makes me wonder if there's a 'have your cake and eat it too' approach to this, so to speak.

lavender is individualism, is me.
